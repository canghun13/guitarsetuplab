Field guide

How to Measure Guitar Control Hardware

Measure mounting holes, panel stacks, shafts, component envelopes, cavities, and harness coordinates from consistent datums.

Separate the interfaces

  1. Disconnect the instrument from amplifiers and power. Photograph every connection before authorized disassembly.
  2. Measure the smallest clear hole or slot, not the screw head or trim ring.
  3. Measure the full panel stack crossed by the threaded bushing, including plates, washers, and finish where relevant.
  4. Measure the component body, terminals, wire bend, and inserted plug as separate envelopes.
  5. For a harness, choose one origin and axis direction, then record every hole center as x,y in that same coordinate system.

Compare current drawings

Names such as import, US, short shaft, long shaft, Strat, Tele, or Les Paul are not dimensions. Use the current candidate drawing and the real instrument. A calculator pass is followed by a physical overlay or dry fit.

Start with the pot mounting checker, then check the knob interface, switch envelope, jack and plug space, or complete harness layout.

Safety boundary Stop for abnormal resistance, cracking sounds, visible movement, electrical hazards, or measurements you cannot reproduce.

Scope of this guide

Measure mounting holes, panel stacks, shafts, component envelopes, cavities, and harness coordinates from consistent datums. Use How to Measure Guitar Control Hardware to make a repeatable decision for this named task, not to impose one specification on every instrument.

Practical example

A nominal short-shaft pot can still fail because the guitar presents a thick carved-top stack, while a long bushing can fit the hole yet place the knob too high. Record the hole, panel stack, usable thread, shaft interface, body, terminals, and cavity independently.

Record the conditions

For How to Measure Guitar Control Hardware, write down instrument type, tuning, installed strings, relevant hardware, climate when material, physical datum, unit, tool resolution, and the result before and after change.
