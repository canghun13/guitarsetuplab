Guitar Recording Level & Latency Reference

Define dBu, dBV, Vrms, dBFS, headroom, samples, milliseconds, and microphone arrival delay.

dBu
A voltage level referenced to 0.775 Vrms; 0 dBu equals 0.775 Vrms.
dBV
A voltage level referenced to 1 Vrms; 0 dBV equals 1 Vrms.
Vrms
Root-mean-square voltage. Convert a voltage ratio to decibels with 20 × log10(ratio).
dBFS
A digital meter scale whose ceiling is 0 dBFS. It is not interchangeable with dBu or dBV without calibrated hardware information.
Headroom
The level difference between the measured or expected peak and an entered maximum.
Sample delay
A count of digital samples between matching transient positions; milliseconds equal samples ÷ sample rate × 1000.
Acoustic arrival delay
Time from a source to a microphone capsule. At 20 °C this reference uses approximately 343.42 m/s.
Phase at one frequency
Arrival delay multiplied by frequency and 360 degrees. It does not describe broadband room response.

Nominal, peak, and maximum specifications answer different questions. Record the exact device, connection, meter mode, sample rate, firmware/software state, and manufacturer source.

Scope of this reference

Define dBu, dBV, Vrms, dBFS, headroom, samples, milliseconds, and microphone arrival delay. Use Guitar Recording Level & Latency Reference to make a repeatable decision for this named task, not to impose one specification on every instrument.

Practical example

0 dBu is 0.775 Vrms, while 0 dBV is 1 Vrms and 0 dBFS is a digital ceiling rather than an analog voltage. At 48 kHz, 480 samples equal 10 ms; the same 480 samples last a different time at another rate.
