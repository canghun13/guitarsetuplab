Pickup Wiring Terms Reference

Plain-language definitions for conductors, series, parallel, split, phase, ground, shielding, and switch poles.

Conductor
An insulated wire carrying a coil connection. A shield or ground braid is not automatically an independent coil conductor.
Series
Coils connected end-to-end; usually the fullest, highest-output humbucker mode.
Parallel
Both coils remain active in a lower-output, clearer arrangement that can retain hum canceling when wired correctly.
Coil split
One coil is bypassed. It is not the same as parallel and usually does not retain full hum canceling.
Electrical phase
Relative signal polarity. Thin combined sound can be a phase clue, but height and output mismatch must also be checked.
Shield
A conductive enclosure tied to the circuit reference. Shielding does not fix every ground or environmental noise problem.

Scope of this reference

Plain-language definitions for conductors, series, parallel, split, phase, ground, shielding, and switch poles. Use Pickup Wiring Terms Reference to make a repeatable decision for this named task, not to impose one specification on every instrument.

Practical example

A four-conductor humbucker can expose individual coil starts and finishes, but the manufacturer’s colors must be mapped before series, parallel, or split switching is planned. Magnetic polarity and electrical phase are related observations, not interchangeable terms.
