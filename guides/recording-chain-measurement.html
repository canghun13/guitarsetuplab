How to Measure a Guitar Recording & Reamp Chain

Capture comparable level and timing evidence for DI, reamp, pedal insert, and microphone paths.

Use one controlled reference

  1. Turn monitoring and device outputs down before changing any connection.
  2. Record a clean DI with a repeatable passage and a distinct transient; preserve the take unchanged.
  3. Use the same DI/interface input, gain, meter mode, and cable path when comparing original and reamp loopback levels.
  4. Read nominal and maximum levels from the exact interface, pedal, DI, or reamp documentation.
  5. Measure source-to-capsule distance from the same acoustic datum and verify arrival in the waveform.

Keep electrical and acoustic timing separate

Round-trip converter or insert delay is measured in samples between matching transients. Microphone arrival delay comes from physical distance and speed of sound. Both can be displayed in samples, but they describe different parts of the path.

Start with the DI loading checker, then use the reamp level planner, pedal insert checker, or timing tools as the unresolved question requires.

Never connect an amplifier speaker output to a DI, reamp box, pedal, or audio-interface input unless the exact manufacturer documentation explicitly provides a compatible speaker-level load/input.
Safety boundary Stop for abnormal resistance, cracking sounds, visible movement, electrical hazards, or measurements you cannot reproduce.

Scope of this guide

Capture comparable level and timing evidence for DI, reamp, pedal insert, and microphone paths. Use How to Measure a Guitar Recording & Reamp Chain to make a repeatable decision for this named task, not to impose one specification on every instrument.

Practical example

A useful reamp check preserves the clean DI, sends it through the intended output/reamp path, and records the returned level through the same DI/interface input and gain used for the original reference. A separate sharp transient supplies sample alignment evidence.
